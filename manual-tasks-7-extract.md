## 3. Introduction
SAAS' key responsibilities related to manual tasks and other Work Health and Safety (WHS) legislation is to manage manual task activities to prevent and reduce the risk of workers being injured.

While the WHS Act and Regulations are the Australian Government's legal framework, the codes of practice form the guidelines on the 'how to'.

Under the WHS Act and Regulations a worker is obliged to use equipment provided and follow procedures and other workplace instructions. Examples of using equipment would include wearing a hard hat when a patient is trapped in a vehicle and using slide sheets rather than lifting patients.

## 4. Act and Framework
Work Health and Safety is a framework that fosters a safe and healthy working environment for all workers:
- Those who manage or control things that create health and safety in the workplace are responsible for eliminating or reducing the risks, so far as is reasonably practical.
- Employers should be proactive in promoting health and safety in the workplace.
- Workers are entitled and should be encouraged to be represented in relation to health and safety issues. Health and Safety Representatives (HSRs) are elected by their peers to represent them with WHS issues.
- Workers and employers have legal responsibilities under the Work Health and Safety Act 2012 (SA). Employers: Section 19.

Click the buttons for more information on the Act. Two pop-ups:

**WHS Act 2012, Section 28 - Duties of Workers.** While at work, a worker must:
- take reasonable care for his or her own health and safety; and
- take reasonable care that his or her acts or omissions do not adversely affect the health and safety of other persons; and
- comply, so far as the worker is reasonably able, with any reasonable instruction that is given by the person conducting the business or undertaking to allow the person to comply with this Act; and
- co-operate with any reasonable policy or procedure of the person conducting the business or undertaking relating to health or safety at the workplace that has been notified to workers.

**WHS Act 2012, Section 29 - Duties of Other Persons at the Workplace.** A person at a workplace (whether or not the person has another duty under this Part) must:
- take reasonable care for his or her own health and safety; and
- take reasonable care that his or her acts or omissions do not adversely affect the health and safety of other persons; and
- comply, so far as the person is reasonably able, with any reasonable instruction that is given by the person conducting the business or undertaking to allow the person conducting the business or undertaking to comply with this Act.

The term 'worker' includes employees, volunteers, students and contractors.

(The sections are quoted as the lesson gives them; I fixed "responsible care" to "reasonable care" in section 28 and "Work and Safety" to "Work Health and Safety". Check the wording against the Act before relying on it.)

## 5. SafeWork SA
The Hazardous Manual Tasks Code of Practice are the WHS guidelines to identify, manage and control manual tasks risks that could result in workers sustaining an immediate or cumulative 'body stressing' injury.

'Body stressing' or 'musculoskeletal' injuries or disorders (MSDs) are the most common workplace injuries across Australia.
