## 1. Manoeuvring Principles (introduction, view.php?id=23493&forceview=1)
- Video: https://player.vimeo.com/video/851515940?h=2bcbb46267

**Five key principles of manoeuvring**

1. **Do not dry steer.** This is when you turn the steering wheel of a vehicle whilst the vehicle is stationary. It shows that the area for the chosen manoeuvre is probably very tight. Doing this increases the wear and tear on the vehicle and the road surface.
2. **Do not hold steering on full lock.** Steering on full lock is when the steering wheel is turned to the maximum extent it can be to either the left or right. Having to hold the steering on full lock shows evidence that the driver is hoping for the vehicle to turn in the space, rather than it being a calculated manoeuvre. It also increases the wear and tear on the vehicle.
3. **Be aware of the pivot point.** A vehicle's pivot point is the area in which a vehicle turns. It is located just in front of your rear wheel. This is the part of the vehicle that has the smallest turning circle. In a longer vehicle, turns have to be made slightly wider or a little later to account for the rear pivot point being further back from the driver.
4. **Face the wheels.** Facing the wheels is when you approach the end of one manoeuvre you set the front wheels of your vehicle towards the next direction of travel. This prevents dry steering and also reduces the turning radius when you commence the next part of the manoeuvre.
5. **Always set up for entry.** On the approach to a tighter area into which you need to drive you position the vehicle a bit wider before turning so that when you enter the area you are parallel.

You will be assessed on this through the completion of a 'cone course' and your ability to perform them on road. Each of these manoeuvres are ones that you will perform regularly on road. ## 3. Cone Course (pageid 1433)
- Video (example run with a text overview of the key principles): https://player.vimeo.com/video/855611246?h=b9fa66400a

As part of your practical assessments, you will be required to undertake a cone course. This involves you demonstrating a series of manoeuvring principles one after the other. While this course is timed, the goal of it is to complete each of the manoeuvres correctly, utilising all of the techniques you have been taught as part of this course.
