## 3. Mobility Risk Assessment
**General information.** SAAS has seen several injuries to staff and patients when patients unexpectedly collapse during transfers or walking. This is a general guide only to aid your decision making. Clinical factors may necessitate alterations in technique.

**Goal:** to discern how much the patient is able to assist you prior to attempting to move them. This will help you to determine: the best technique to use, the number of staff and any equipment you will need to perform the task safely.

For example, it is unwise to try and stand a patient who can't weight-bear reliably or try to sit someone up in bed if they have poor trunk control. These more dependent patients are likely to need slide sheets, Hover Matts, compact chairs, etc. to move them safely, as they can't help much.

**Precautions.** Prior to conducting a mobility risk assessment, information on the patient's recent medical history and medication will alert the clinician if the patient may become dizzy with sitting up or standing, with potential to collapse.

## 5 to 7. Knowledge Check: Mobility Assessment IN A Bed
Three slides. Each stem: "Patient is lying in bed and you decide to do a mobility risk assessment before you move the patient." Choose the assessment tool from: **Head lift / Bridging / Straight leg raise**.
- If you are checking the leg strength and coordination of the patient, you are asking the patient to demonstrate: **Straight leg raise** (inferred).
- If you are checking the trunk control and hip extensor strength of the patient, you are asking the patient to demonstrate: **Bridging** (inferred).
- If you are checking head control and if their head needs support during transfer, you are asking the patient to demonstrate: **Head lift** (inferred).

## 8 to 10. Knowledge Check: Mobility Assessment IN A Chair
Three slides. Each stem: "Patient is sitting on a chair and you decide to do a mobility risk assessment before you move the patient." Choose from: **Reaching outside the base of support / Marching on the spot / Leg extension**.
- If you are checking their quads' strength and leg coordination, you are asking the patient to demonstrate: **Leg extension** (inferred).
- If you are checking their strength, coordination and balance, you are asking the patient to demonstrate: **Marching on the spot** (inferred).
- If you are checking their trunk control and balance, you are asking the patient to demonstrate: **Reaching outside the base of support** (inferred).
