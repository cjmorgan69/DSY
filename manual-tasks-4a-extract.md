## 2. What Is A Stryker Stretcher, and Why Do We Use A Stryker Stretcher?
The Stryker stretcher and power load system were introduced to SAAS in 2017 to replace the manual Ferno 2650 model. Injuries with manually raising and lowering the Ferno stretcher and physically loading in and out of a vehicle have significantly reduced with the powered functions on the stretcher and the power load system.

All stretcher functions, loading and unloading are covered in the practical training.

There are also Stryker procedures on SAASnet that cover the content of the videos.

## 5. Assisted Raising Stretcher With Patients Weighing 226-318 kg
The hydraulics of the stretcher can struggle with patients weighing over 226 kg. Staff may need to assist the stretcher to raise it to a height where it can be raised to load into the ambulance. Always try replacing the battery first.

PRO-338 describes how you can safely undertake this task.

## 6. Special Applications: Pedi-mate and Safety Net
The Pedi-Mate harness is used to secure small children on the stretcher. This is covered during practical training and instructions on use are documented in PRO-255 Stryker Power-PRO Stretcher and Power-LOAD Procedure.

The safety net is used to restrain non-cooperative patients during transportation. This is to prevent the patient putting themselves and workers at risk by attempting to remove stretcher safety belts and move about during transportation.

## 8. Manoeuvring a Laden Stretcher
Moving a laden stretcher can be hazardous if the patient is very heavy and the ground surface is soft, sloped or uneven. A second person needs to steer the stretcher and safe techniques are covered in the practical course and demonstrated in the videos.

Good communication between partners is essential, especially if negotiating narrow hallways in hospitals and aged care facilities.

Another hazardous task is lifting or lowering stretchers up or down curbs. When a curb cannot be avoided there is a recommended technique for both persons to share the load.

## 9. Stretcher to Bed Transfers (Non-Ambulant Patients)
The lateral transfer is one of the most frequently used transfers for Ambulance Officers. It is the safest method to transfer a patient who cannot stand from the ambulance stretcher to a hospital bed/stretcher. It involves use of anti-friction devices to lessen the push pull forces as well as lessen the friction on the skin of patients. This transfer normally involves four workers, two Ambulance Officers and two nursing staff and must be well coordinated to avoid injury to staff and patients.

This transfer is extensively covered and is a competency requirement of practical training and assessment.
