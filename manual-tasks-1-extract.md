## 8. Functions of the Human Body: Skeletal Muscles
Three steps, shown 1 / 3 to 3 / 3:

**1 / 3.** Skeletal muscles are the main muscles we think about with movement of the body. These are voluntary muscles that not only work together to provide movement but provide postural support. Muscles are very adaptable: they can get stronger and bigger with exercise or atrophy with disuse; they can contract or relax and work with bones and joints to provide lever system.

How you use skeletal muscles will protect the body from injury, especially the spine and shoulders.

Using the back and upper back 'core muscle' groups in conjunction with the stronger leg and buttock muscles allows weightlifters to protect their body when lifting heavy weights.

**2 / 3.** Workers must apply the same principles to work efficiently and safely at all times.

Stronger leg muscle groups used are (click on the buttons to view each type of muscle group; this activates the next slide button): calf muscles; quadriceps; gluteal muscles. (The pop-ups for each group show a picture with a one-line label only.)

**3 / 3.** Other muscles in the body are 'involuntary', i.e. operate without conscious control.

These involuntary muscles include cardiac, and smooth muscles (as found in the digestive, respiratory organs, eyes and blood vessels).

Click on the body parts to reveal what types of muscle they have (picture hotspots; no text stored).

## 9. What Injuries Can Manual Tasks Cause?
These are some of the most common activities that can lead to injury, that occur at work as an Ambulance Clinician.

Click the buttons to select the correct manoeuvre for each category. The Next button will not display until the correct manoeuvre is selected for all categories. For each, the learner picks the correct picture ("Please select an image that you believe is the correct manoeuvre") and then sees Don't versus Do:

| Activity | Don't | Do |
|---|---|---|
| Prolonged sitting / slouching in a chair | Sit in a slouched posture while working at the computer as it places stress on your spine and corresponding muscles. | Adjust the chair, monitor and keyboard to encourage best posture. Regularly stand and stretch. |
| Moving an empty stretcher | Drag the stretcher with one hand, it can lead to shoulder injury. | Push empty stretcher with two hands. |
| Checking kits | Placing the kit on the ground can lead to straining the calves, knees or back. | Place on passenger seat to check if it's a tall person, or on floor in vehicle by sidestep if short person. |
| Exiting vehicle front cabin | Leap out of the front seats of the vehicle, it can lead to knee and ankle injury. | Face inwards when descending from the front seat, maintaining three points of contact. |
| Closing the vehicle door/s | Hurriedly close the back or side door with one hand, it can lead to elbow/shoulder/spinal injury. | Use two hands to close the door in a controlled manner, following the door as it closes. |
| Carrying and handling equipment | Overload your body by carrying too many items of equipment at once. | Maintain a balanced and supported posture to protect your body from injury. |
