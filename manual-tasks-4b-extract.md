# Part 1: Compact Carry Chair

## 1. Compact Carry Chair (title)
Approx. 30 min.

## 2. Compact Carry Chair
The compact carry chair, or 'green chair', is a standard item of SAAS equipment. It is used to transport patients who cannot, or should not, walk to the Stryker stretcher due to their medical condition.

The chair is narrow and therefore can access areas the Stryker stretcher cannot get close to. Patients with suspected spinal fractures, poor sitting balance or reduced consciousness, or greater than 115 kg should not be transported on this chair.

# Part 2: Spinal Board

## 9. Spinal Board (title)
Approx. 10 min.

## 10. Spinal Board General Guidelines
Two text panels (1 / 2 and 2 / 2) and one video:

**1 / 2.** The spinal board is mainly used to drag or lift and carry an injured patient when spinal precautions need to be observed. It can be used as a sliding bridge between floor and stretcher as discussed later, or as a flat surface to help move a patient out of a wrecked vehicle.

Dragging the spinal board using belts to pull and steer the board is less risky than lifting a patient on a spinal board.

With lifting and carrying patients on a spinal board the weight of the patient and the distance moved determines the number of workers who should be used to make the lift safe. For example, a patient under 100 kg could be safely moved using four workers, but a patient over 150 kg would need six workers lifting to make the transfer safer.

The spinal board used by SAAS has a safe work load of 220 kg, but where possible very heavy patients are moved using less risk techniques and other equipment. The Bariatric Team and Special Operations Team have specialist equipment and training to manage moving very heavy patients or in complex retrievals that are not time dependent, or at least be available to give advice over the phone.

**2 / 2.** Often SAAS staff are lifting patients on spinal boards with non-ambulance workers. For this reason, it is important that there is a leader nominated to coordinate the lift to determine everyone lifts at the same time and all are clear about moving together.

Team lifting, and especially lifting off the ground, is a high-risk task. Workers should use the 'weight lifters' posture to best protect themselves when lifting, this is demonstrated in the video.

## 14. Blue Slide Sheet (title)
Approx. 10 min.

## 15. What is a Blue Slide Sheet?
A blue slide sheet is used to move supine patients by sliding rather than lifting them.

Being a slippery material, the force to move a patient is less using this manual handling accessory than attempting to move the patient without it. It is useful for moving patients in a congested area where other equipment cannot be safely used and including out of lounge chairs.

## 16. Sliding the Patient On the Blue Slide Sheet
Two steps (1 / 2 and 2 / 2):

**1 / 2. Head End First.** A patient is normally slid headfirst. If the patient is hypotensive, i.e. low blood pressure, sliding feet first is recommended.
- Secure a metal buckled strap to the corner of the head end of the blue slide sheet making these belts as long as possible.
- Ensure path to stretcher is clear.
- Two clinicians move the patient by lunging backwards with their elbows locked into their sides.
- If the patient must be moved around corners a third person can steer from the foot end using an elongated plastic buckle belt attached to the foot end loops.
- In restricted spaces where it is not possible to move a patient flat, walking forward to shorten the leading end straps will shorten the load and allow you to negotiate turns and tight spaces.
- Video "Manoeuvring patient sitting up": SAAS Blue Slide Sheet - Manoeuvring Sitting Patient Up (20230620).mp4 (0:57): https://saaslearn.com.au/moodle/pluginfile.php/15321/mod_scorm/content/5/story_content/video_5Y5K47rZW9C_22_48_640x360.mp4

**2 / 2. Raised Feet End First.** If the person has low blood pressure this is the preferred method as the patient's legs can be raised during the transfer.
- The metal buckle straps are attached to the foot end blue slide sheet loops.
- The steering belt is attached to the head end loops.
- The foot end belts are kept as long as possible as with the headfirst method, but if the patient becomes hypotensive, the patient's legs can be raised by both clinicians at the foot end walking towards the patient and shortening the straps.
- Video "Manoeuvring patient with legs raised": SAAS Blue Slide Sheet - Manoeuvring Patient Legs Raised (20230620).mp4 (0:50): https://saaslearn.com.au/moodle/pluginfile.php/15321/mod_scorm/content/5/story_content/video_6kvMRuM7hFB_22_48_640x360.mp4

## 17. Manoeuvring Patient in Restricted Space
In restricted spaces where it is not possible to move a patient whilst laying flat, secure a weight bearing belt (steel buckle) to each corner at the head end of the slide sheet. Attach a securing belt through both loops at the foot end of the slide sheet.

When you need to move through a confined area, shorten the straps, as you walk towards the patient, bringing the patient into the sitting position, then using the backwards lunge, negotiate the confined area, carefully laying the patient down as belts are lengthened to continue sliding the patient in the supine position.

## 18. Stretcher Bridge Transfer
Lifting patients from the floor onto a stretcher is a high-risk task for workers and can cause further injury to the patient. Using a spinal board as a 'bridge' to slide patients secured in a blue sheet up onto the stretcher from the foot or head end is a useful and safer technique than lifting the patient.

## 19. Transferring an Unconscious Patient to the Floor
Moving an unconscious patient from a chair or bed to the floor is recognised as a high-risk task as it can be time critical for airway and clinical management. The 'chair' could be a recliner, toilet or couch.
- Quickly remove obstacles to create enough space on the floor for the patient to be lowered to and attended to.
- Adopt a strong posture. If the person is wedged in the seat the patient may have to be rolled slightly to move them in stages.
- Control their head as they are moved to the floor.
- If working in a restricted space, consider lowering the patient onto a blue slide sheet to use to pull them a short distance into an open space.
