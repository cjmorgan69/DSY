## 4. Larger or Bariatric Patients
Patients are becoming larger with 31% of adult Australians rated as obese and 65% as overweight or obese.

SAAS crews are handling larger patients than previously, given the increased capacity of the Stryker stretcher.

While an independent larger patient is a low risk to transport, managing a dependent heavier patient will increase the risks of worker injury.

While the medical definition for bariatric patients is having a body mass index (BMI) of 40 or greater, safe handling depends on multiple factors other than patient weight which include:
- The safe working load of equipment to transport and lift the patient
- The patient's body shape and level of function
- Their medical condition and response time required
- Staff resources available (SAAS and other emergency responders)
- The environment

## 5. Patient Sizing
Many larger patients can be transported on the Stryker stretcher. However, the limiting factor is mainly the person's body shape, especially their width with fitting on the stretcher and into the back of an ambulance. Inside the ambulance, between the wall and rear attendant seat, the maximum width is 700 mm when the patient is secured on the stretcher.

The most common bariatric body shapes are the Apple and Pear. Click on the buttons to view information on each type of bariatric body shape:
- **Apple:** Weight is carried high. Fat stored around the abdomen. May have difficulty lying flat.
- **Pear:** Weight carried below waist. May have a wide base stance.
- **Anasarca:** Severe generalised oedema.
- **Bulbus gluteal region:** Excessive buttock tissue creating a protruding shelf.

## 7. Introduction to Bariatric Equipment
The following information will help you learn more about tools that help you lift or move patients safely. Tools like this eliminate the need for us to lift or exert ourselves, greatly reducing the potential for injury.

Camels and Raizers are commonly used as additional equipment items. Hovermatts, Hoverjacks and Stair Chairs are specialised equipment used by the Metro bariatric team. Larger Regional sites have access to Hoverjacks and Hovermatts.

Click on the buttons to view more information about each item of equipment (three buttons, with panels below):

**CAMEL Lifting Device.** Designed to sit up and lift a person from the floor, either independently or assisted. It is used to help patients from the ground when it is not safe to use other techniques. SWL is 320 kg.
- Procedure (internal SAAS SharePoint): Using a Lifting Device to Get a Patient Up Off the Floor - CAMEL SWL 320 kg, RAIZER LIFT 150 kg [PRO-323]
  https://sagov.sharepoint.com/sites/SAAS/policies/Documents/PRO-323%20Using%20a%20Lifting%20device%20to%20get%20a%20patient%20up%20off%20the%20floor%20-%20CAMEL%20SWL%20320kg%20%20RAIZER%20LIFT%20150kg%20-%20INTRANET.pdf

**Ferno EZ Glide Evacuation Chair** (1 / 2). The Ferno EZ Glide Evacuation Chair is designed to 'glide' seated patients down stairs without having to carry or lift. Large 15 cm rear wheels and 10 cm front swivel wheels allow the chair to roll easily over various surfaces and low obstacles. Appropriate manual tasks training in addition to the standard course is necessary before using the Ferno EZ Glide. SWL is 227 kg. Equipment weight: 15 kg.
(2 / 2) A powered chair was recently purchased for the ESS team that can transport patients up and down stairs or steep ramps. The manual EZ Glide Evacuation chair is used in Mount Gambier.

**HoverMatt and HoverJack** (two panels, 1 / 2 and 2 / 2):
- **HoverMatt.** The HoverMatt is used when moving bariatric patients in order to move them from one location to another along the ground. Sections of rubber matting can be laid and re-laid to create a path over which the HoverMatt can be moved. SAAS crews can use the HoverMatt at the patient's residence or at a hospital when sliding patients from one bed to another, or to and from the ambulance stretcher. The mat has tiny holes on the underside surface and when connected to a compressor that blows air, the mat inflates slightly and pushes air out the holes, creating a 'hover' mechanism. The patient can then be moved in the same manner as a non-bariatric patient would be on a blue slide sheet.
- **HoverJack.** The HoverJack is a series of stacked 'airbeds' inflated in succession to raise a bariatric patient from ground level to the level of a bed or stretcher.
