Pressure immobilisation technique (PIT)

Watch this demonstration of the pressure immobilisation technique.

Key points

  • Bandage firmness: similar to a sprained ankle
  • Coverage: entire limb from fingers or toes to armpit or groin
  • Overlap: each loop covers 2/3 of the previous loop
  • Circulation check: only cuticle beds visible
  • Mark the bite site with a pen on the bandage
  • Keep the patient still and immobilised
  • Do NOT remove jewellery before bandaging
  • Do NOT apply tourniquets

Bandaging guide and calculator

Use this calculator to estimate bandaging requirements.

Standard equipment

  • SA Ambulance Service Snakebite Bandage Kit (4 x 15cm elasticised roller bandages)
  • Large cardboard splint for immobilisation
  • Triangular bandages or general purpose roller bandages
  • Vacuum mattress (preferred for transport)
  • Pen for marking bite site

6

Key principles and what not to do

Do

  • Call emergency services (000) immediately
  • Keep the patient as still as possible
  • Apply pressure bandaging over the bite site
  • Extend bandaging to the entire limb
  • Mark the bite site on the bandage with a pen
  • Immobilise the limb with a splint or vacuum mattress
  • Keep the bitten area below heart level
  • Monitor for signs of cardiovascular collapse

Do not

  • Apply a tourniquet (cuts off blood circulation)
  • Wash or clean the bite site (needed for venom identification)
  • Cut the wound or attempt to suck out venom
  • Apply ice directly to the wound
  • Remove jewellery before bandaging (do it before swelling)
  • Attempt to catch or kill the animal
  • Allow the patient to walk
  • Remove pressure bandages without medical advice
