## 5. Manual Tasks Techniques: Postures and Actions
Click on the buttons below to learn about the different postures and actions. Five buttons, each with a 1 / 2 description panel and a 2 / 2 video:

**Weightlifter's Posture**
- Video (0:47): Part 11.mp4: https://saaslearn.com.au/moodle/pluginfile.php/15317/mod_scorm/content/4/story_content/video_6GQtgYqMwsy_22_48_604x340.mp4
- The weightlifter's posture is used to lift loads close to the ground. This can be a difficult posture to master, but it is important to avoid injury. One example of its use is to lift a laden spinal board from the ground.
  - Feet shoulder width apart, flat on the ground
  - Bend at the hips, stick your bottom out
  - Maintain your natural spinal curves
  - Don't let your hips drop below your knees

**Knight's Position**
- Video (0:37): Part 10.mp4: https://saaslearn.com.au/moodle/pluginfile.php/15317/mod_scorm/content/4/story_content/video_5dEh3AV9eYS_22_48_604x340.mp4
- The Knight's position is similar to a proposal posture with the front foot flat on the ground and your front knee bent at 90 degrees.
  - Front foot flat on ground, knee at 90 degrees
  - Back leg kneels on the floor

**Forwards and Backwards Lunges**
- Video (0:40): Part 9 new.mp4: https://saaslearn.com.au/moodle/pluginfile.php/15317/mod_scorm/content/4/story_content/video_6bYLhXZCaae_22_48_604x340.mp4
- The forwards and backwards lunges require a shift of bodyweight through a single plane, with the legs providing power.
  - Split stance with feet hip width apart
  - Transfer of weight to move the load, don't pull or push with the arms
  - Hand grip supinated for backwards lunge
  - Hand grip pronated for forward lunge

**Sideways Lunge**
- Video (0:41): Part 8 (2023).mp4: https://saaslearn.com.au/moodle/pluginfile.php/15317/mod_scorm/content/4/story_content/video_5qeP2J1IVcB_22_48_604x340.mp4
- The sideways lunge is completed by shifting your body weight from side to side.
  - Feet slightly more than shoulder width apart
  - Knees soft and slightly bent
  - Hands supinated, elbows close to the body
  - Transfer weight, but move along the same plane

**Bench Posture**
- Video (0:44): Part 7 new.mp4: https://saaslearn.com.au/moodle/pluginfile.php/15317/mod_scorm/content/4/story_content/video_6KFaK5GLODO_22_48_604x340.mp4
- The bench posture looks like a semi-squat position.
  - Feet hip-width apart
  - Poke your bottom out
  - Maintain your natural spinal curves
  - Keep your toes pointing forward
  - Knees travel in the same direction as toes

## 10. Checking Equipment
To avoid stooping or working off the floor, always place equipment on a surface that is the appropriate height for you when restocking kits or checking equipment.

Suitable surfaces can include the front seats of the vehicle, the natural step created inside the side door, the adjustable height stretcher (removed from the back of the ambulance and covered with a sheet to protect the clean linen), and existing work benches.

## 16. Do Not Exit Via the Back Door
You must not enter or exit the ambulance from the rear doors.

You can use the rear door step to assist you to reach the rearward lockers, or to safely load/unload equipment, but three points of contact must be maintained at all times.

Procedure link (internal SAAS SharePoint): Ascending and Descending Ambulance Steps [PRO-306]
https://sagov.sharepoint.com/sites/SAAS/policies/Documents/PRO-306%20Safe%20Ascending%20and%20Descending%20Ambulance%20Steps%20-%20INTRANET.pdf

## 17. Corpuls Cardiac Monitor Handling and Storage
Four steps (1 / 4 to 4 / 4):

1. **Storage and Charging.** The Corpuls cardiac monitor will be stored and charged in a Corpuls bracket in front of the rear attendant seat. When not in use, the Corpuls cardiac monitor should be stored in its bracket and turned to face the front of the vehicle for easy access.
2. **Rotating Bracket.** To rotate the bracket and Corpuls, stand near the side door and rotate the Corpuls bracket by pulling the pin upward (located at the front of the bracket). Rotate the monitor screen to face the front of the vehicle. Positioning the monitor in this position before lifting it or returning it to the bracket will prevent overreaching and twisting in your body.
3. **Removal.** To lift the monitor out of its bracket, disengage the bracket locking mechanism (red handle) by lifting it upward. With your other hand lift the Corpuls unit off and away from the cradle, keeping your elbows close to your sides and standing close to the bracket to avoid overreaching.
4. **Return.** To return the Corpuls monitor to the charging bracket tilt the monitor using two hands and line up the monitor base with the locking points.

## 18. Applying Postures and Safe Lifting
Consider the following scenarios and choose how you would make sure you are working within your A-zone. Click the buttons to view more information on each scenario. The Next button will not display until the scenarios are viewed. In each, the learner picks the correct manoeuvre (picture choice) and then sees the feedback:

| Scenario | Question | Correct | Incorrect |
|---|---|---|---|
| One | Which technique would you choose to recover an item from a high shelf in the storeroom? | You are using the correct manual handling technique and equipment. By using the steps the items on the shelf will be in your A-zone. | The items will still be in your B-zone, which can result in shoulder injury. |
| Two | Which technique would you choose to clean the stretcher? | The stretcher will be at a comfortable height within your A-zone. | By lowering the stretcher it will be in your B-zone. You risk injuring your knees or back in this position. |
| Three | Which technique would you choose to open the rear door of the ambulance? | Moving with the door reduces the stress on your body by keeping the door in your A-Zone. | The weight of the door applies stress to your body in the limbs, joints and spine. |

(So: scenario one is to use steps, scenario two is to raise the stretcher, scenario three is to move with the door. The pictures show which option is which.)
