## 1. Introduction to Policies, Procedures and the Law
- Video: https://player.vimeo.com/video/863440908?h=fbeb97ac89

A clear understanding of your responsibilities regarding SAAS policies and procedures is vital. It is expected you will read and understand the content of the following Policies and Procedures and your responsibilities within them. We have provided a short summary of some DRiVE relevant points, but you will need to review the Policies and Procedures for further essential context and information relevant to your role within SAAS and to complete the Theory Assessment.

## 2. Policy - Driver Responsibilities and Requirements [POL-022] (pageid 1793)
- Image: Pol 22 Driver responsibility.JPG

Key points of this policy. Drivers must:
- Be correctly licenced to drive SAAS vehicles
- Drive in a professional manner at all times
- Undertake training as required
- Present their driver's licence to a manager upon request at any time and in accordance with the Drivers Licence Audit procedure (PRO-181)
- Use seatbelts at all times in the front of the vehicle
- Use all vehicle safety devices as required

## 3. Policy - Zero blood alcohol and zero illegal drug levels when driving [POL-035] (1794)
- Image: POL-035 Zero blood alcohol and Zero illegal drug levels when driving.jpg

Key points of this policy. When driving SAAS vehicles, either marked or non-marked, for any business or operational need, SAAS requirement is to:
- Drive with a zero blood alcohol level
- Drive with a zero saliva level of any illegal drug
- Not be under the adverse influence of any prescription drug

## 5. Procedure - Reversing Vehicles [PRO-072] (1795)
- Image: pro 72 reversing vehicles.JPG

Key points from the procedure:
- Staff should do all they can to reduce any reverse driving.
- The driver is required to request the attendant to assist in the reverse manoeuvre if not involved in patient care.
- When reversing, have windows down and do not deactivate the reverse beeper unless being guided back in an enclosed space.
- Reversing must always be done at a slow speed.
- Use all reversing aids possible, including additional lighting.
- The attendant, when sitting in the front, has an obligation to assist even if not asked.
- The driver will not move the vehicle if any uncertainty exists.

## 6. Procedure - Driving and Fatigue [PRO-104] (1807)
- Image: pro 104 driving and fatique.JPG

Key points of this procedure:
- All staff are required to be compliant with this procedure.
- Know what the signs of fatigue are and know how to report.
- In SAAS, long-distance driving is defined as a single journey that is greater than two continuous hours of driving.
- Each period of two continuous hours of driving by the same driver will be broken by a break of at least fifteen minutes.
- With regard to operational ambulance driving, the driver and attendant should rotate their duties to ensure that no more than two hours of consecutive driving is completed by any SAAS staff member. It is noted that in almost all circumstances it is clinically appropriate for a crew to share the primary care of a patient.
- No individual staff member should be required to drive a SAAS vehicle in excess of eight total hours of driving (excluding breaks), during any 24 hour period.

## 7. Procedure - Processing of Expiation Notices and Alleged Driving Offences [PRO-240] (1797)
- Image: pro 240 processing of expiation.JPG

Key points of this procedure. Client Relations will gather the SACAD record and Patient Clinical Record (PCR) (if required) and compare to the expiation notice information. Client Relations check the expiation is within the following criteria:
- A confirmed emergency tasking recorded in SACAD as per PRO-055 Emergency Driving
- Warning devices are active in accordance with ARR 306
- The expiation shows a speed within PRO-055 Emergency Driving (no more than 30 km/h over the posted speed limit)
- Red light camera activation speed of less than 20 km/h into an intersection
- The expiation does not relate to a rail crossing controlled by an activated red light

If the above criteria are met, Client Relations are to seek exemption from SAPOL in accordance with ARR 306. All records relating to the expiation will be stored on file for SAAS reference. If one or more criteria are not met, Client Relations refer the expiation to the staff member (driver) to clarify the circumstances of the expiation OR request the notice be reissued to them personally via the Expiation Notice Declaration Form (ENDF).

## 12. Procedure - Assistance Dog Transport [PRO-199] (1802)
- Image: pro 199 Assistance dog transport.JPG

Key points from this procedure:
- **The rule/act that supports SAAS to transport accredited dogs:** the Disability Discrimination Act 1992 (DDA) section 23, part (a), makes it unlawful for a person with a disability to be refused access to, or the use of, any premises. For SAAS' purposes, this includes operational patient carrying ambulances.
- **Dog placement in ambulance:** ensure that the rear attendant seat has been moved as far forward as possible. The assistance dog is then prompted to jump up and sit behind the attendant seat.
- The SAAS supplied tether (located in rear door locker) is attached to the dog's harness or collar and then to the Douglas tracking embedded in the floor of the ambulance.
- Assistance dogs must be tethered to their existing harness as a first choice, or alternately to their collar if the dog is not wearing a harness.
- Accredited assistance dogs in South Australia are issued with an identity card which the handler can be requested to present.

## 13. Procedure - Safe Storage of Patient Luggage and Equipment [PRO-202] (1801)
- Image: pro 202 safe storage.JPG

Key points from this procedure:
- **Non-wheeled fold up walker:** walking frames (that fold flat) may be carried by securing the frame behind the front passenger's seat. By winding the seat back on the frame, ensure the frame and/or vehicle interior is not damaged.
- **4 wheeled walker:** a folded 4 wheeled walker can be secured on the vacant rear attendant airway seat with the handles facing away from the driver. Place a sheet on the attendant airway seat to protect it and secure the frame using the seat belt. An additional metal buckle belt must be used to secure the walking frame to the seat back.
- **Additional equipment / patient belongings:** at the rear of the vehicle, use the cargo net to secure in the Douglas tracking.

## 17. Road Traffic Act Section 45 and 46 - Exemption versus Defence (1799)
- Image: RTA.JPG

Key points from the Road Traffic Act:
- There are many driving offences that an emergency vehicle driver is not exempt from. Offences under sections 45-46 of the Road Traffic Act are laws that cannot be dealt with by means of an exemption under Australian Road Rule 306:
  - 45: Careless driving
  - 45A: Excessive speed
  - 46: Reckless and dangerous driving
- Instead of an exemption, a defence may be applicable if you were to be charged under any of the above.
- In order to be able to claim a defence you have to prove three things:
  - (a) carrying out duties as an emergency worker; and
  - (b) acting in accordance with the directions of the defendant's employing authority; and
  - (c) acting reasonably in the circumstances as the defendant believed them to be.
- If you drive outside of the policy and procedures that SAAS has stipulated, then you are not following a direction of the employing authority, which may lead to you not being able to claim a defence to the charge.

## 18. Australian Road Rules (1806)
- Image: ARR.JPG

**ARR 306 and what is 'reasonable?'** Key points of this road rule:
- Australian Road Rule 306 is the law that gives drivers of emergency vehicles the ability to drive using emergency provisions.
- It has 3 key requirements:
  - the driver is taking reasonable care; and
  - it is reasonable that the rule should not apply; and
  - if the vehicle is a motor vehicle that is moving, the vehicle is displaying a blue or red flashing light or sounding an alarm.
- All these factors must be considered for every emergency driving decision that is made.

What is reasonable: It is no good causing stress, damage or injury to other persons just for you to be able to get to a patient. We owe a duty of care to all persons on the road. Negligence is a tort, which is committed when someone with a duty of care fails to act reasonably, and someone else consequently suffers injury, loss or personal damage. If your actions are not reasonable then you may be found negligent. So how do we determine what is reasonable as we drive using emergency provisions along a road?
- We should not affect the safety of our partners and work colleagues.
- We should not affect the safety of other persons on the road.
- We should not affect the safety of other persons or property in road related areas.

If your driving action forces another road user to swerve off the road or brake hard that could be seen as not reasonable. We cannot stipulate what is and is not reasonable for every situation you will come across when driving. If you go about your driving with a zero-harm mindset, then your actions will most likely be seen as reasonable.

**'Right of way' and ARR 78/79.** Key points of this road rule:
- Right of way is a common externalising excuse for unsafe driving actions.
- Under Australian Road Rules there is no right of way, only the obligation to give way.
- ARR Readers Guide: There are a number of rules requiring a driver to give way to another driver or a pedestrian. However, under the Rules the other driver or pedestrian does not have a "right" of way. Indeed, in some situations, a number of drivers may be required to give way to each other, e.g. at an intersection with a stop sign or give way sign on more than 1 of the intersecting roads.
- Being in an emergency vehicle does NOT give you right of way over others, even if you are displaying a red and blue flashing light. Others should give way to you, but it does not give you the right of way!

Rule 78, Keeping clear of police and emergency vehicles:
- A driver must not move into the path of an approaching police or emergency vehicle.
- If a driver is in the path of an approaching police or emergency vehicle the driver must move out of the path of the vehicle as soon as the driver can do so safely.
- This rule applies to the driver despite any other rule of the Australian Road Rules.

Rule 79, Giving way to police and emergency vehicles:
- A driver must give way to a police or emergency vehicle that is displaying a flashing blue or red light or sounding an alarm.
- For this rule, give way means: if the driver is stopped, remain stationary until it is safe to proceed; or in any other case, slow down and if necessary, stop to avoid a collision.
- This rule applies to the driver despite any other rule that would otherwise require the driver of a police or emergency vehicle to give way to the driver.

It is important to note that these two rules could cause confusion for other road users, and we see very regularly whilst emergency driving.
Thought: If stopped at a traffic light with an emergency vehicle behind them, should a car move or remain stationary? We must never force others into taking risky manoeuvres or we may breach ARR 306 (reasonable).

**ARR 307.** Key points of this road rule:
- ARR 307 is the rule that deals with stopping and parking exemption for police and emergency vehicles and authorised persons.
- A provision of Part 12 does not apply to the driver of a police vehicle or emergency vehicle if, in the circumstances: the driver is taking reasonable care; and it is reasonable that the provision should not apply. (Review 'reasonable' under the 306 section.)
- We must only use this exemption for times when we are parking on a dispatched case.

**ARR 305.** Key points of this road rule: This emergency driving exemption rule is only applicable to the Police. It is equivalent to the rules under ARR 306. ARR 305 does have some further exemptions that only the police can use.

**ARR 129/130, Lane choice.** Key points of these road rules:
- On an un-laned road the driver must drive as near as practical to the left side of the road.
- On a multi-lane road if speed limit is over 80 km/hr then you must keep to the left.
- On a multi-lane road if a "keep left unless overtaking" sign applies then you must keep to the left.
- Other lane choice considerations: as much as possible on a dual-lane carriageway, ambulances should be driven in lane 2 (right lane). On multiple lane roads the ambulance should be driven in the lane that provides the best vision to the left and right.
- When negotiating turns we need to make the corner as wide as possible (to assist stable platform) so use outside lanes if legal to do so, or position wide in your lane.
- Using an outside lane because it is "smoother" is not a reasonable excuse if a road law makes it an offence to do so. If you need to travel in an outside lane when it is not legal to do so, you must consider being able to claim an exemption under rule ARR 306.

**ARR 264, 265, 266 and 267, Use of seat belts.** Key points of these road rules and SAAS policy:
- All ambulance personnel must maintain SAAS operational policy and procedure requirements, road laws written in the RTA and the ARR and WHS legislation to ensure their own safety and wellbeing and that of their passengers / patients whilst travelling in a SAAS vehicle.
- Exemptions may exist under law in particular circumstances, however SAAS staff are not to go unrestrained unless you are carrying out a lifesaving intervention in the rear of the vehicle.
- The correct fitment of your seatbelt: it should be low on hips and low across shoulder; it should be flat with no twists; it should be firm.

**ARR 300, Use of mobile phone.** Key points of this road rule and SAAS policy:
- ARR 300 makes it an offence to use a handheld mobile phone device whilst driving.
- SAAS has phones fitted into a cradle that are affixed to the vehicle. The phone must not be removed from the cradle by the driver of a vehicle that is not parked.
- In some circumstances under law an emergency worker could claim an exemption to rule 300, however SAAS policy states: the driver of a SAAS vehicle shall only use a SAAS issued mobile phone that is fitted into a hands-free vehicle kit when operationally essential or when related to a patient's clinical condition.
- The driver of a SAAS vehicle shall not use any function of a personal mobile phone whilst driving.
