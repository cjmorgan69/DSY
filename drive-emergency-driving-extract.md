## 4. Dispatch Priorities
Generally, when leaving a scene attended as an emergency, you do not have to drive from that scene under emergency conditions:
- Sufficient reason must exist for an emergency transport: threat to life or limb.
- Must advise Emergency Operations Centre.
- Must ensure a stable environment and reasonable care.

Priority 1: Emergency. Immediately life-threatening. Requires an emergency response.
Priority 2: Emergency. Increased risk of mortality or morbidity. Requires an emergency response.

## 6. Emergency Driving Speed
Things to consider when choosing a speed:
- 'Zero Harm' principles.
- SAAS policy and procedure requirements.
- Needs of your partner and the patient.
- Conditions of the road and surrounding environment.
- How your driving will be portrayed by the public.

## 7. SAAS Speed Policy
- No specific speed 'policy'.
- SAAS cannot tell you what a 'safe speed' is for every set of conditions.
- You must have evidence that the speed you were driving was reasonable.
- Expiation Notice procedure:
  - Never drive more than 30 km/h above a posted speed limit.
  - Never drive faster than 20 km/h through an intersection where you are faced with a red light.

## 8. Pedestrian Crossings
Pedestrian crossing red lights are to be treated with the same speed reduction as intersection red lights, with the addition that we must wait for any pedestrians to get off the road or be safe in a median strip refuge before we proceed.

## 9. Parking at Scene and Safe Speed Zone
Main slide:
- Approach slowly and allow yourself time to assess the situation.
- Give an overview SITREP, if possible.
- Consider where and how you park in accordance with Australian Road Rule 307.
- Make an assessment of: access and egress; access for others; ground integrity; hazards.

Five clickable topics: Australian Road Rule 307; Parking Considerations; Emergency Speed Zone; Height Restricted Carparks; Steep Driveways.

**Australian Road Rule 307** (1 of 3 in the parking group). In certain circumstances we can claim an exemption to some parking regulations. It is important to note that SAAS has deemed that we must be on an active case and parking at that scene for this to apply. The exemption comes under Australian Road Rule 307.

**Parking Considerations.** Parking in areas other than a solid concrete or bitumen surface can present problems whilst parking. The Mercedes ambulance tyres are relatively narrow with a high pressure which makes them susceptible to sinking into soft surfaces. The vehicle's drive mechanisms are not designed for soft loose surface operation. This should be considered when faced with parking at sporting fields for example as the risk of getting stuck is real.

**Emergency Speed Zone.** To make a road scene as safe as possible the first vehicle should always park in the fend off position to protect the scene. Any subsequent emergency vehicles should park beyond the scene and then that area becomes a safe speed zone and 25 km/hr applies to all vehicles passing from the first resources to the last. Both vehicles must be displaying red or blue flashing lights for this to apply.

**Height Restricted Carparks.** When either travelling in forward or reverse you must always be aware that you are in a vehicle that is over 2.5 metres high. This must be taken into consideration when travelling into residential care facilities, carparks, hospitals or reversing in areas with lower buildings and trees. There are no high-level sensors to warn of overhanging objects when manoeuvring.

**Steep Driveways.** Private and business driveways present challenges in the fact that sometimes upon entering the driveway we cannot determine the final parking/turning location. Steep driveways present another issue in that these generally compound blind spot issues and should be treated with extreme caution. If you cannot see the end point upon entering the drive, stop and check prior to progressing onto the driveway.
Other factors when entering driveways that can cause issues for our drivers and require extra caution to be taken include overhanging property eaves, letter boxes, fences, low retaining walls, garden ornaments, furniture and other vehicles already parked at site. Many of these elements will be in the vehicle's blind spots when manoeuvring.

## 11. Patient Carry Emergency Driving
When you have to travel using emergency provisions with a patient on board there are several things that must be done and or considered:
- Just because you have travelled to a scene using emergency provisions does not automatically mean that you travel from the scene to the hospital using emergency provisions.
- Sufficient medical reason must exist.
- As previously discussed in the Mindset section, the driver's bandwidth will become very full.
- Communication with the attending clinician will be increased.
- A stable platform will be required to allow for essential treatments to be continued throughout the journey.
- Excessive braking, steering and acceleration will compromise patient care.
- Some options like going over curbs should not be used.

Legal and operational requirements:
- The PH (priority to hospital) button must be pressed on the MDT (top left corner after pressing "mobile" and entering destination).
- Communicate on GRN radio with EOC to advise of emergency drive.
- Prepare for a notification to the hospital.

## 13. Mobile CPR
If you are driving whilst CPR is taking place in the vehicle, the safety of the persons unrestrained is of paramount importance and as such your scanning, looking, forecasting, and planning must be at a very high level. Communications with persons in the back must be clear and advise of any driving actions that may cause loss of balance. It is important that the ambulance is driven appropriately to ensure effective external cardiac compressions (ECC) can occur en-route (simulations suggest that ECC effectiveness rapidly decreases in speeds greater than 25 km/hr) (CPG - Cardiac Arrest).

Use of lights and sirens during this slower type of emergency drive can cause confusion to other road users. It is important to use these safety devices effectively to ensure a consistent and smooth journey whilst minimising confusion.

Consideration should be given to using mechanical chest compression devices (MCCD) where available as this eliminates the risk of having an unrestrained clinician and reduction of ineffective ECC. However, driving must still facilitate a stable platform for the device to work optimally.

## 19. Overtaking and Trucks
When coming up behind a heavy vehicle, if we have not been seen in the distance, we are likely to be in the rear blind spot of the truck. This combined with the driver not being able to hear the siren can lead to the heavy vehicle not moving out of the path of the emergency vehicle. In this scenario move to the right and left of the lane and change siren tones to try and get the truck driver's attention in their side mirrors.

A truck driver may not be able to move to the left for several reasons and may give some indication acknowledging your presence. In this case, leaving the siren active, efficiently move down the left-hand side of the vehicle.

If the driver has not seen or acknowledged you, using a dynamic risk assessment looking carefully at the road ahead and surrounding infrastructure and other road users, consider overtaking on the left. If doing this manoeuvre and the truck driver is not aware of the emergency vehicle presence, consider turning your siren off when commencing passing the vehicle on its left. This is to reduce the chance of getting halfway down the vehicle, the truck driver hearing the siren and on doing so commencing a left lane change without fully checking their blind spot to the left.

Reactivate the siren once past the vehicle and return to position of advantage.

## 20. Overtaking and Trucks (2: example thoughts)
The driver's running thoughts, as three steps:
1. "I don't think the driver has heard or seen me. I have tried different tones and rumbler. I think I will move to the right to try and get into their wing mirrors."
2. "The driver has still not seen me, the road ahead looks straight and clear. I will try the left."
3. "The driver has still given no sign that they know I am here. The road ahead to the left of the truck is clear. I am going to de-activate my siren and start to pass on the left."

## 21. Median Strip, Gutters and Curbs
- Video (in the Median Strip Curbs panel, showing the techniques): https://player.vimeo.com/video/860017233?h=9e8c649020

There are many things to consider when driving on median strips, gutters and curbs including their shape, width and surface, and vehicle clearance. Click on each of the buttons to view information about these considerations. Buttons: Median Strip Curbs; Vehicle Clearance; Width and Surface; North Terrace Tram Curb.

**Median Strip Curbs.** If considering crossing a median strip one of the first decisions is what type of curb edging it has. It is preferred to only use sloped curbs as these present much less tyre wall intrusion. If a square curb must be considered this would have to be negotiated at less than walking pace with the tyres turned towards it at quite an acute angle. Negotiating curbs with a patient on board is strongly discouraged due to the impact it has on the vehicle's stable platform. (Pictures: square curb, sloped curb.)

**Vehicle Clearance.** When crossing a median strip, it is vital to remember that we have very limited clearance under the vehicle and if the incorrect median strip is chosen to cross this can lead to significant damage to the vehicle and make it un-operational. The road on either side of the median strip may not be the same height on either side of the curb and this must be considered in your planning when crossing. Although we may have just enough clearance to get over a raised median strip in a slow-moving vehicle, if any additional speed is added we may induce more suspension travel and therefore bounce the vehicle onto the curb lining. As can be seen by the attached pictures the side step and rear differential are two very susceptible areas of damage.

**Median Strip Width and Surface.** Another consideration when deciding to cross a median strip is the width and its structure. The ideal median strip to cross has sloped curbs and is wide enough to get two wheels up, four wheels up, two wheels down, four wheels down and is made of a solid substrate. A median strip that is not wide enough to accept the whole vehicle may present an option to just place two wheels up onto the median strip whilst travelling forward. These techniques are demonstrated in the video. (1 of 2)
Wider median strips that have grass or vegetation must be treated with caution as if wet they can cause traction problems for the Mercedes vehicle. The Mercedes vehicles have a relatively narrow tyre with a high pressure which makes it more susceptible to getting bogged. Median strips that are narrower than the width of the vehicle will lead to the vehicle straddling the curb line which increases the chance of damage dramatically and should be avoided. (2 of 2)

**North Terrace Tram Curb.** There is a safety alert (SAF-18-004) that stipulates not driving eastbound on the median strip (tram track) outside Parliament House on North Terrace. This median strip (tram track) has a 42 cm drop off and signage in the centre of the median strip that forces a driver to drive very close to that drop off edge. Going over the edge of this area could result in the ambulance becoming stuck or potentially rolling over.

## 23. Road Works
Considerations when approaching road works:
- Lower posted speed limits. Reduce your overall speed.
- Workers on and near the road may have hearing protection in place and may not hear a "normal" siren. So, the use of the rumbler if fitted may be effective.
- Workers may be operating machinery which creates block outs and may not be aware of your presence.
- Traffic will be driving in narrower lanes and may be on broken surfaces.
- Additional direction may be being given by road workers on site.

## 25. Animal Interactions - Horses
In many areas where we drive, or close to some ambulance stations, we may interact with a variety of animals, animal related industries or domestic animal interactions. Extreme care must be taken when emergency driving near animals as a startled animal can be difficult and dangerous to control.

Consideration to the use of the siren needs to be thought through carefully. General principle: if the siren is active and you note animals in the distance with your scanning, it is best to leave the siren on but do not change the tone type until you are safely past. Activating the siren near the animal is strongly discouraged due to the risk of startling the animal and injuring the handler and/or animal. If animals are noted in the distance during the scanning pattern and you elect to turn the siren off, your driving must be modified as you are now not using one of your safety devices.

## 26. Special Access Locations
When tasked to a case we may have the ability to access certain areas where the public cannot go in a vehicle. Access to areas may be restricted by an electronic boom gate and access or use of these needs to be arranged through the Emergency Operations Centre or follow local arrangements.

Two clickable topics:
- **Post Bollards.** Post style bollards can restrict access to many public areas, and these can be controlled either electronically with keypads, by local facilities management operation or by audio communication at the site of the bollards.
- **Lift Barriers and Gates.** Other access restriction devices could include lift out barriers and gates both manual or electronic. It is important to remember if you open a manual gate or lift barrier that it is replaced once you have passed through.

## 28. Causative Factors
A causative factor crash is when a member of the public's vehicle has given way to an emergency ambulance as required by Australian Road Rule 78. This vehicle has then been impacted by a second public vehicle which has either failed to give way or not left a safe driving distance as required by Australian Road Rule 126.

What to do if involved in a causative factor crash:
- Contact EOC to be taken off the case originally attending.
- Check all persons at scene of crash.
- Exchange required particulars.
- Contact SDM for incident number.
- Report to Police (if they do not attend the scene).
- If any injury has occurred reporting must be done within 90 mins.
- If no injuries occurred reporting must be within 24 hours.
