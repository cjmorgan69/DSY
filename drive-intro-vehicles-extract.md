## 2. OUTS and OUTSET (pageid 1413)
- Video: https://player.vimeo.com/video/855543433?h=ebb8465807

Vehicle checks are carried out to ensure the vehicle is mechanically safe and legally roadworthy. The driver is responsible for the vehicle they are driving.

OUTS is performed every time you approach and start the vehicle.
- **O** Walk around the vehicle and check for external damage/defects that have not been reported in the logbook; check for excessive dirt build up, particularly windscreen and lights.
- **U** Under: check underneath the ambulance for evidence of oil, coolant, water, transmission fluid leaks; attempt to identify source of the leak and report to Fleet or SDM.
- **T** Tyres: check the tyres appear in good condition and appear to be correctly inflated (pressure may be able to be done via electronic reporting in vehicle, newer vehicles have individual tyre pressure shown whereas older style just detect a 2psi difference between the tyres and then shows a low tyre pressure warning). Ensure legal tread is evident using the tyre wear indicator.
- **S** Start vehicle and check dash symbols. Action any items that are displayed on the dash as required.

The OUTSET check is performed at the commencement of a shift or as soon as possible after the start of the shift. It is also completed when you change into a different fleet during a vehicle swap. It has additional steps to that of OUTS.
- **O, U, T, S** as above.
- **E** Electronics: a full review of vehicle lights and systems to ensure functionality.
- **T** Tools of the trade: inspect and test medical equipment, stretcher and communication equipment. Explain usage of MDT, radio, sat phone, UHF radio.

## 3. Workshop and Damage (pageid 1414)
- Video: https://player.vimeo.com/video/851181886?h=7cfce7f48b
- Images: Location of Damage and Repair Book with Highlight.png; Vehicle service and repair book.JPG; Vehicle service and repair book filled in.JPG

If damage isn't reported then it can't be fixed.

All SAAS fleet have a Vehicle Service, Repair and Defect Report Book which is usually located in the passenger side footwell.
The defect and repair book should be filled out by entering the details at the top of the page and then a description of the fault in the top section of the page. Fleet will complete when the vehicle has been assessed. When you have filled in a defect and repair request it is important that if this defect affects the safe or legal operation of the vehicle, that it is escalated to the SDM/Fleet by phone as soon as possible. If the defect or repair is of a minor nature this information can be documented in the book and left for the next service session.

**For metro ambulances**
Where a vehicle fault needs to be attended to immediately the EOC Team Leader needs to be notified and arrangements made for the vehicle to be brought into Fleet Services for rectification. Fleet Services will attempt to repair any faults on a priority basis minimising downtime whilst the crew remains on site. Where the fault cannot be repaired in a reasonable time the crew will be requested to change over into a spare vehicle. On completion Fleet Services will notify the EOC the vehicle is ready to be returned into service.

**For country ambulances**
All minor repairs are to be recorded in Vehicle Fault Book; these repairs must be done during normal vehicle servicing. However if the repair required is urgent attention, the following needs to occur:
- Book vehicle into authorised service agent for service / repair.
- Complete a Vehicle repair authorisation form and email it to Fleet Services using the email link provided including: Service Agent's name; Fleet number; Vehicle location; Current odometer reading; Work to be carried out; Member's name and pay number; Return fax number.
- Fleet Services will place a purchase order number on the form and fax back to nominated Service Agent.
- Fleet Services will raise work order in Fleet Management System.
- Status of the work order in the fleet system will be changed to complete.

## 4. Go No-Go (pageid 1415)
- Video: https://player.vimeo.com/video/855553302?h=773810e0d1

You must be able to make the Go No-Go decision as to whether a vehicle is safe and legal to use based on available evidence.

The Go No-Go decision must be made by the driver post the vehicle check to determine whether to use the vehicle or not. Guiding principles are:
- Anything that affects the safe or legal operation of the vehicle: it should not be used. **No-Go**: the state duty manager should be contacted for advice before vehicle use (generally along with Fleet Services advice).
- If issues identified are not affecting the safe or legal operation of the vehicle: **Go**. Ensure these are documented in the fleet servicing book and contact your Team Leader and Fleet Services to arrange for the item to be repaired at a convenient time.
- If an issue does affect the safe or legal operation of the vehicle: post discussion with SDM/TL a decision may be made to use the vehicle, but it may have some restrictions on its use, i.e. no emergency driving, no patient carries etc.
- As the driver of the vehicle, the individual carries a large percentage of the responsibility both operationally and legally when it comes to the vehicle safety and carrying out appropriate vehicle checks. If in doubt never be afraid of asking the question.

## 7. Starting Issues (pageid 1418)
- Video: https://player.vimeo.com/video/851168532?h=39fb7e6b8c

There are several reasons why it may be difficult to start a vehicle. Listed below are some of the issues with their solutions, however some issues can only be dealt with by fleet.
- Activating the ignition and nothing happening: maybe the result of the weather cover on the charging port not being properly closed. If this does appear to be closed press the silver override button located at the left base of the driver's seat whilst starting the ignition and then let it go.
- Starting the ignition and hearing a slow turnover or ticking: could mean a low starter battery. In this case locate the emergency start button on the panel on top of the dash, press the button to couple the batteries and start the vehicle.
- Activation of the ignition and nothing happening, also accompanied by a red light on the battery indicator panel: means the battery is flat and requires a jump start. The cables for this are located under the airway seat and should be connected as shown in the video.

## 11. Beacons/Travel (pageid 1422)
- Video: https://player.vimeo.com/video/855572286?h=73166da7ce
- Video: https://player.vimeo.com/video/851490865?h=1ec7031d56

Emergency lights "beacons" should be checked during the vehicle check. It is difficult to fully check the lights on your own as for the white lights to be active the handbrake must be off which means that it is not safe to get out of the vehicle.

"Beacons" are the preferred lighting for emergency driving, this means all the red and blue lights along with the white light will be active. On our vehicles, white light can be seen flashing at a greater distance than the red or blue lights.

"Travel" mode is to be used for when driving in fog or when you have multiple reflective signage on the edge of the road. When operated, no flashing / strobing white lights are illuminated so you do not get white light throwback.

As white light can be disruptive while on scene, if your vehicle is in "beacons" mode when you apply the handbrake, the white light gets turned off.
If parked at remote scenes with aircraft activity you may be required to turn off all emergency lighting. This is due to use of night vision goggles and the strobing lights being too bright and compromising a pilot's vision.
The static light above the number plate is there to indicate on an expiation notice that the emergency lights are active (a requirement of rule 306) so that exemption can be claimed.
Sometimes even this static light does not show up and that is due to the pulse frequency of LED type lights. To the naked eye they do not flash but when viewed through a camera the strobing / flashing effect can be seen. Always ensure these lights are operating during your vehicle check.

## 12. Siren Operation (pageid 1423)
- Videos: https://player.vimeo.com/video/855573500?h=a7a418ecd0 ; https://player.vimeo.com/video/855583635?h=f98ff5e6e1 ; https://player.vimeo.com/video/855584053?h=ff61676333
- Image: Close up foot pedles.JPG
- Procedure link: Emergency Driving [PRO-055] https://sagov.sharepoint.com/sites/SAAS/policies/Documents/PRO-055%20Emergency%20Driving%20-%20INTRANET.pdf (intranet)
- Audio (base https://saaslearn.com.au/moodle/pluginfile.php/31597/mod_lesson/page_contents/1423/): Siren Wail.WAV; Siren Yelp .WAV; Siren Combo.WAV; Rumbler and Wail.wav; Rumbler and Yelp.wav; Rumbler and Combo.mp3

If you are driving a vehicle under emergency response conditions and interacting with moving traffic then your siren should be on. Ensure you understand the use of sirens as per emergency driving procedure.

Standard siren use. The sirens in most of the fleet have three distinctive tones: wail, yelp or combined wail / yelp. As a guide to the type of siren to use and when:
- Use the **wail** on long stretches of road as this sound carries the furthest.
- Use the **yelp** on the approach to an intersection or congested area.
- Use the **combined** siren as you negotiate the 'Hazard' area.

The siren can be changed by using the foot switch located at the forward end of the foot brace (preferred method). In the VS30 fleet you also switch the siren by quickly tapping the vehicle horn.

The siren is a safety device and careful consideration must be given to your driving style if you elect to turn it off at any time you are engaged in emergency response driving.

Rumbler siren use. This is a deep bass siren and will run for 9 seconds when activated. If siren tones are changed during this time it will change to tone of the rumbler. The rumbler is only available on VS30 model ambulances and is activated by firmly pressing the horn twice in quick succession.
The rumbler should be considered when you are in the presence of:
- Road workers with hearing protection on
- Person wearing crash helmets
- Pedestrians that appear distracted or not aware of your presence (maybe deaf or headphones?)
- Vehicles that have failed to hear the normal siren

Depending on your device audio output, the deep bass of the rumbler may be difficult to hear.

## 13. Speed Limiter and Cruise Control (pageid 1424)
- Video (Mercedes, adaptive speed control and safety features): https://player.vimeo.com/video/855584849?h=299ffa823d

Three features of the Mercedes that can be used whilst driving are cruise control, the speed limiter and adaptive cruise control.

**Cruise control** will allow the vehicle to maintain a set speed without application of the accelerator. It is only recommended to use cruise control on open roads. Do not use cruise control in more built-up areas when you have patients on board. The reason is that it takes away the driver's ability to use accelerator sense and will translate into a more uncomfortable ride for the patient and your partner.

**Speed limiter.** The speed limiter in a Mercedes can be set to any required speed and will physically stop you going over that speed in normal circumstances. The use of the speed limiter during emergency driving can assist in managing driving within the confines of the emergency driving procedure.

**Adaptive cruise control.** Please review Mercedes video attached that introduces adaptive speed control and other safety features of these vehicles.

## 14. Seat Setup and Seat Belt (pageid 1425)
- Videos: https://player.vimeo.com/video/851480946?h=d2aa4c2149 ; https://player.vimeo.com/video/851186176?h=d5af9c31c5 (the second sits under "Steering wheel setup")

The reason for ensuring that your seat is set up correctly is so that the driver has the "best control and safety" and to "ensure all controls can be accessed effectively".

Seat setup. Correct seat adjustments will also ensure the safe and comfortable driving posture which will in turn lessen driver distraction. The following steps should be completed in order:
1. **Left foot brace.** Place your foot against the brace, so that you can push against it when required. By pushing against the brace and pushing back on the steering wheel the driver will be able to brace their body to assist in accurate steering around corners and in the event of an adverse incident occurring.
2. **Bend in knee.** Adjust the base of the seat forward/backward and up/down, so you have a slight bend in your knee in the braced position. When relaxed, the knee bend will naturally increase slightly. The driver's foot should always be on the brace but in a relaxed position during drives, so it is there when needed.
3. **Deep in seat.** Push your backside deep, back and down into the seat. If you cannot do this without easing your foot off the brace then the seat will need to move forward. The brace will not be as effective if you cannot brace against the back of the seat. When bracing, if the driver finds themselves pushing upwards off the seat, then a lower seat height adjustment will probably have to be made.
4. **Hand position.**
   - The steering wheel should be held at either the 9 and 3 position, or the 10 and 2 position. Consideration has to be given to steering wheel design and controls on the steering wheel for the best hand positioning.
   - The height of the steering wheel should be as low as possible to keep the shoulders relaxed to decrease neck and shoulder strain issues. The driver should position the wheel to be able to see the top of the dash display just under the rim of the top of the steering wheel.
   - For the correct "distance" of the steering wheel, there should be a gentle bend at the elbows when holding the wheel correctly.
   - Thumbs should be "in": give a person a pen or drink bottle to hold, they will use their thumb. Effective control of steering requires correct holding. Try holding the drink bottle without using the thumb, it does not feel correct. The only time thumbs should be up the wheel is when a person is driving off road on rough terrain.
   - No palm steering is allowed. Palm steering has control but is carried out with one hand and any slippage could lead to not negotiating the obstacle safely. There is no margin of error, so it is not to be done.
5. **Firm shoulder contact.** When holding the steering wheel correctly apply some light bracing with a slight push back on the wheel, your shoulders should firmly contact the seat without the driver fully straightening their arms. As an additional check, get the driver to slide their hands around the wheel as if they were doing a full turn. This motion should not make the driver lean forward and lose contact with the back of the seat.
6. **Head restraint.** The top of the head restraint should be positioned a little above the top of the ears. When you push your head back, the restraint should not cause it to pivot up or down.
7. **Suspension seat.** A suspension seat requires you to adjust the counter spring to your desired comfort. Some people prefer to have a seat really "springy" but if too light a weight selected it could lead to the seat "bottoming" out and this causes a sudden stop which could be detrimental. Alternatively setting the counter spring too firm will not allow much movement and therefore the ruts and road surface will be felt more through the seat. Using the "weight" dial as a guide I have found setting the weight to about 20-30 kg above my actual weight is the comfortable setting for me. This is something everyone will have to adjust and find what works for them. If you do not like the seat moving it can be "locked out" so that there is no movement. This requires the use of the lateral lever on the front base of the seat to be flicked over whilst there is compression on the seat.

## 15. Mirror Setup (pageid 1426)
- Video: https://player.vimeo.com/video/851485078?h=9d719c31cf

The controls for the outside mirrors are on the driver's door, next to the electronic window control. The top 'main' mirrors are heated automatically in cold weather to prevent them fogging. The lower mirror is convex and will reduce the area that is known as a blind spot, but it will not eliminate the blind spot. Objects in this mirror appear smaller and closer than their actual size or position.

The internal centre mirror offers an obscured external rear view. It is more use being able to sight your partner and patient, than the obstructed view out of the back windows. At night when the internal lights are being used, this mirror has no ability to be used for any external view and can cause a 'light' distraction to the driver's field of forward vision.

## 17. Preparing for Night Driving (pageid 1428)
- Video: https://player.vimeo.com/video/851486991?h=9c063b09a8

Preparing yourself and the vehicle for night driving is essential. You should ensure that you are rested and not fatigued prior to commencing your shift.
- The vehicle must have clean windows, mirrors and lights. Dirty rain drops or vehicle wash residue on the external rear view mirror may not be seen as a problem during daylight hours, but at night they significantly reduce your clear view.
- Dirt on the outside or inside of the vehicle's windscreen can deflect light and cause the outside view to be hazy which reduces your ability to see your driving environment clearly, and increase your chance of being involved in a driving incident.
- Make sure ALL lights work during your vehicle check.
- Use window demister on the window setting, air conditioner on and fresh air input (not recycled air) to keep windscreen clear.
- Keep internal lights as low as possible to reduce internal light reflection on the windscreen, which in turn will enhance external vision (use curtain behind driver's seat if fitted).
- If reversing in the dark (not recommended) use a person to guide you where possible. It may sometimes help to turn headlights off (but leave side lights on), as this will help increase your rear vision. Use the rear/side scan lights to light up the guide/area into which you are reversing.

## 18. Moving Familiarisation (pageid 1430)
- Video: https://player.vimeo.com/video/851488987?h=e952c85f02

**Tap, Tap, Wiggle, Wiggle**

The moving familiarisation is the process of checking the accelerator, brake and steering as you move off your initial drive.

To complete this, you apply initial pressure to the accelerator to find the positive drive point and then apply the brakes so that you can feel the pressure needed to move the pedal and discover where the pressure starts after any possible pedal slack.
Then as the vehicle moves off you turn the steering wheel to the left and right, this will ensure you have steering and discover any slack in the movement of the wheel. It will also help ensure that you have set the wheel up correctly for control and comfort.

## 19. Navigation Options (pageid 1429)
- Video: https://player.vimeo.com/video/855591435?h=f8b89e24ab
- Image: Giving Directions-01.png

Most of the SAAS vehicles are fitted with GPS units that are linked to the Mobile Data Terminals (MDT) and will self-populate when tasked to a case. On some occasions you may be required to load information into a separate GPS or manually add to the one associated with the MDT. In some parts of the State it may not be possible to get a clear and steady GPS signal or occasionally this equipment might not be operational and the Emergency Operations Centre (EOC) will dispatch with map co-ordinates. This could be from a UBD or CFS map book.

**Maps and map books.** Given all of the electronic tools available for navigation, it will be rare that you will be required to use maps or map books. However, these rare occasions do happen so all ambulance personnel must be able to quickly and accurately give directions from a map book. All staff must be able to read UBD, CFS, topographical and state road maps; and understand RPA and Rapid numbers and be able to locate them.

**Giving navigation directions.** Giving map direction to the driver must be in the following method: **AT-DO**
- Info 1: At the end of the road, turn left. (Wait until this manoeuvre is completed, then:)
- Info 2: After the roundabout take the first street on the right. (Wait until this manoeuvre is completed, then:)
- Info 3: At the fourth street on the left, turn left, the one before is Smith Street. (Wait until you turn in, then:)
- Info 4: Number 18 on the left.

Use hand directions to assist in driver's peripheral vision. The reason we do this is that this method of information does not overload a driver whilst they are concentrating on routine or emergency driving and it complements the method of scanning that we use. If we were to say "turn left at the roundabout", as soon as the driver hears turn left, they will look for the left turn. If we say, "at the roundabout turn left", the driver's vision will go forward to the roundabout and not seek an immediate left. During an emergency drive the vehicle cabin is a busy and noisy place so accurate and timely navigation cues are very important.

Know how to use RPA (rural property addressing), rapid numbers and grid references to read state maps ("cross the river, climb the tree").

**Rural property addressing.** Rural Area Property Identity (RAPID) numbers are no longer being issued however some properties may still display the yellow plate with the six figures on it that may look like a vehicle number plate and is often posted on the entrance to the property. These six-figure grid references relate to a 1:50 000 topographical map for that area.
In South Australia a system exists where property in rural areas can be easily located using the rural property addressing system, these numbers are jointly managed by the state government and local councils. The format of the sign is determined by the local council area. This consists of:
- A distance-based road number
- Road name
- Locality
- Postcode

Property numbers are worked out based on the distance from the start of the road to the entrance of the property. That distance, in metres, is divided by ten. Even numbers are on the right and odd numbers are on the left. For example, the entrance to a property 5,080 metres from the start of the road on the right hand side becomes number 508. The start of the road is determined as the fastest and safest road accessed from the nearest major road or town. Rural road maps define the name, the start point and direction of every rural road in the state.

**Grid references.** Grid references are used to pinpoint a location on the map. Overlaid across the map is a grid made up of vertical lines running north south and horizontal lines running east west. The first numbers in a grid reference are always across the map. As a memory prompt you can use the phrase "cross the river and then climb the tree". Grid squares usually represent one square kilometre. The space between the lines can then be further divided into 10 even spaces to turn the two-digit numbers into three digit numbers. The combination of the three digits across the top (or bottom) and then the three digits down the side gives a "six figure grid reference" for a particular location.
